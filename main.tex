\title[Largest New Eigenvalues of Random Covers]{The Distribution of the Largest New Eigenvalues of Random Covers
of Graphs}
\author{Eric Naslund}
\email{naslund.math@gmail.com}

\begin{abstract}
A finite $d$-regular graph $G$ on $V$ vertices is said to be \emph{one-sided
Ramanujan} if its second largest adjacency eigenvalue $\lambda_{2}(G)$
is at most $2\sqrt{d-1}$, the spectral radius of the infinite $d$-regular
tree. Miller, Novikoff, and Sabelli \cite{MillerNovikoffSabelli2008}
conjectured that the probability of being Ramanujan tends to approximately
$52\%$. Analyzing $1.3\times10^{8}$ samples of the largest new eigenvalue
across families of random regular graphs and random covers, we find
that the $52\%$ was a finite size artifact of power law fits over
small sizes, and that
\[
\lim_{V\rightarrow\infty}P\left(G\text{ is one-sided Ramanujan}\right)=F_{1}(0)=0.8319081\dots,
\]
where $F_{1}$ is the Tracy-Widom distribution of the Gaussian Orthogonal
Ensemble in its original normalization, since proven by Huang, McKenzie,
and Yau. Our main experimental finding
concerns random covers of graphs: the largest new eigenvalue of a
random cover fluctuates according to the Tracy-Widom law of symmetry
class $\beta\in\{1,2,4\}$ selected by the Frobenius-Schur type of
the associated irreducible representation of the covering group, with
centering at the spectral radius of the universal cover of the base
graph, and
the limiting probability that a random cover is Ramanujan is an explicit
product of Tracy-Widom functionals, which the data verifies to three
decimal places.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $G$ be a $d$-regular graph on $V$ vertices with adjacency matrix
eigenvalues $d=\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{V}$.
The spectral gap $d-\lambda_{2}$ controls the expansion of $G$,
and the extent to which it can be large is governed by the spectral
radius $2\sqrt{d-1}$ of the infinite $d$-regular tree. Alon and
Boppana (see Nilli \cite{Nilli1991}) proved that for every $d$-regular
graph on $V$ vertices
\[
\lambda_{2}(G)\geq2\sqrt{d-1}-\frac{C_{d}}{\log V}
\]
for a constant $C_{d}>0$, and so $2\sqrt{d-1}$ is the best spectral
gap achievable by an infinite family. Following Lubotzky, Phillips,
and Sarnak \cite{LubotzkyPhillipsSarnak1988}, a $d$-regular graph
is called \emph{Ramanujan} if every eigenvalue other than $\pm d$
is at most $2\sqrt{d-1}$ in absolute value, and \emph{one-sided Ramanujan}
if
\[
\lambda_{2}(G)\leq2\sqrt{d-1}.
\]
Lubotzky, Phillips, and Sarnak \cite{LubotzkyPhillipsSarnak1988}
and independently Margulis \cite{Margulis1988} constructed infinite
families of Ramanujan graphs when $d-1$ is prime, using deep results
from number theory, and Marcus, Spielman, and Srivastava \cite{MarcusSpielmanSrivastava2015}
proved that bipartite Ramanujan graphs exist for every degree. Whether
non-bipartite Ramanujan graphs exist for every degree remains open.
In a different direction, Alon \cite{Alon1986} conjectured that random
$d$-regular graphs are nearly Ramanujan, that is $\lambda_{2}\leq2\sqrt{d-1}+\epsilon$
asymptotically almost surely for every $\epsilon>0$, and this was
proven by Friedman \cite{Friedman2008}.

Friedman's theorem localizes $\lambda_{2}$ at the Ramanujan threshold
but says nothing about which side of the threshold it falls on, and
Sarnak \cite{Sarnak2004} raised the question of whether a positive
proportion of random $d$-regular graphs are Ramanujan. The first
serious numerical attack on this question was made in 2008 by Miller,
Novikoff, and Sabelli \cite{MillerNovikoffSabelli2008}, hereafter
MNS. Sampling $1000$ graphs per size
from bipartite and perfect matching models of $d$-regular graphs
with $d\in\{3,4,7,10\}$ and $V\leq20{,}000$, with $100{,}000$ graphs
at three selected sizes, they gave evidence that the fluctuations
of $\lambda_{2}$ follow the $\beta=1$ Tracy-Widom law of random
matrix theory \cite{TracyWidom1996}, the law of the largest eigenvalue
of the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble. Writing $\mu_{V}$ and $\sigma_{V}$
for the mean and standard deviation of $\lambda_{2}$ over graphs
of size $V$, they fitted power laws
\[
2\sqrt{d-1}-\mu_{V}\sim c_{\mu}V^{m},\ \ \ \sigma_{V}\sim c_{\sigma}V^{s},
\]
and found best fit exponents $m$ between $-0.79$ and $-0.83$ against
$s$ between $-0.71$ and $-0.73$ for their $d=3$ families. Since
their fitted $m$ was steeper than their fitted $s$, the threshold
$2\sqrt{d-1}$ sits asymptotically at the mean of the distribution,
and they were led to conjecture that the limiting probability of being
one-sided Ramanujan is the mass of the Tracy-Widom law to the left
of its mean,
\begin{equation}
F_{1}(\mu_{TW_{1}})=0.519652\dots,\label{eq:mns_52_percent}
\end{equation}
approximately $52\%$, with $27\%\approx52\%^{2}$ of non-bipartite
graphs Ramanujan on both sides.

The fluctuation question for random regular graphs has since been
answered. For degrees growing with the number of vertices, Bauerschmidt,
Huang, Knowles, and Yau \cite{BauerschmidtHuangKnowlesYau2020} proved
Tracy-Widom fluctuations for $V^{2/9+\mathfrak{c}}\leq d\leq V^{1/3-\mathfrak{c}}$,
He \cite{He2022} for $V^{2/3+\mathfrak{c}}\leq d\leq V/2$, and Huang
and Yau \cite{HuangYau2023} for the full sparse regime $V^{\mathfrak{c}}\leq d\leq V^{1/3-\mathfrak{c}}$,
concluding that about $69\%$ of such graphs are Ramanujan, and correcting
the $27\%$ prediction: the correct one-sided constant is $83\%$,
not $52\%$. For fixed degree, Huang, McKenzie, and Yau \cite{HuangMcKenzieYau2024}
proved the definitive result.
\begin{thm}
\label{thm:hmy}Let $d\geq3$ be fixed, let $G$ be a uniformly random
simple $d$-regular graph on $V$ vertices, and let $A=d(d-1)/(d-2)^{2}$.
Then
\[
(AV)^{2/3}\left(\frac{\lambda_{2}(G)}{\sqrt{d-1}}-2\right)\rightarrow TW_{1}
\]
in distribution as $V\rightarrow\infty$, where $TW_{1}$ is the $\beta=1$
Tracy-Widom distribution in its original normalization. In particular,
since the centering is exactly $2\sqrt{d-1}$,
\[
\lim_{V\rightarrow\infty}P\left(\lambda_{2}(G)\leq2\sqrt{d-1}\right)=F_{1}(0)=0.8319081\dots.
\]
\end{thm}
Unfolding the constants, Theorem \ref{thm:hmy} states that the mean
and standard deviation of $\lambda_{2}$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
2\sqrt{d-1}-\mu_{V}\approx\left|\mu_{TW_{1}}\right|c(d)V^{-2/3},\ \ \ \sigma_{V}\approx\sigma_{TW_{1}}c(d)V^{-2/3},\label{eq:hmy_constants}
\end{equation}
where $c(d)=\left(\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{d(d-1)}\right)^{2/3}\sqrt{d-1}$, and
where $\mu_{TW_{1}}=-1.2065335746\dots$ and $\sigma_{TW_{1}}=1.2679830577\dots$
are the mean and standard deviation of the un-recentered Tracy-Widom
law, so that both of MNS's exponents equal $-2/3$ and the threshold
sits $\left|\mu_{TW_{1}}\right|/\sigma_{TW_{1}}=0.951537\dots$ standard
deviations to the right of the mean.

We report on a numerical study, roughly a thousand times
larger than that of MNS, of the largest new eigenvalue of random covers
of graphs, a setting which contains the random regular graph as its
simplest case and in which the fluctuation theory is entirely open.
A degree $k$ cover (or $k$-lift) $H$ of a base graph $B$ is obtained
by assigning to each edge of $B$ a permutation in $S_{k}$, and the
eigenvalues of $H$ split into the \emph{old} eigenvalues, which are those
of $B$, and the remaining $(k-1)\left|V(B)\right|$ \emph{new} eigenvalues. Random covers were
introduced as a combinatorial construction of expanders by Amit and
Linial \cite{AmitLinial2002}, and Bilu and Linial \cite{BiluLinial2006}
conjectured that every $d$-regular graph has a $2$-cover
whose new eigenvalues all lie in $[-2\sqrt{d-1},2\sqrt{d-1}]$, which
would produce Ramanujan graphs of every degree. Marcus, Spielman,
and Srivastava \cite{MarcusSpielmanSrivastava2015} proved the one-sided
statement for bipartite graphs, and Hall, Puder, and Sawin \cite{HallPuderSawin2018}
proved that every connected loopless graph has a one-sided Ramanujan
cover of every degree. For random covers, Friedman \cite{Friedman2003}
formulated the relative Alon conjecture, that the largest new eigenvalue
of a random degree $k$ cover of $B$ is at most $\rho(B)+\epsilon$
asymptotically almost surely, where $\rho(B)$ is the spectral radius
of the universal cover of $B$, and this was proven by Bordenave \cite{Bordenave2020}
(see also \cite{BordenaveCollins2019}). Accordingly, we call a cover
$H$ of $B$ \emph{one-sided Ramanujan} if every new eigenvalue is
at most $\rho(B)$. When $B$ is $d$-regular the universal cover
is the $d$-regular tree and $\rho(B)=2\sqrt{d-1}$, and when $B$
is the bouquet of $d/2$ loops on a single vertex, a random degree
$k$ cover of $B$ is precisely the permutation model of a random
$d$-regular graph on $k$ vertices, with new eigenvalues its non-trivial
eigenvalues. The relative Alon conjecture is thus the exact analog
of Friedman's theorem, and the question answered by Theorem \ref{thm:hmy}
returns in general form: how does the largest new eigenvalue of a
random cover fluctuate around $\rho(B)$, and what proportion of random
covers are Ramanujan?

Our data supports a precise and, to our knowledge, new answer: the
limiting fluctuations follow the Tracy-Widom law of the random matrix
symmetry class dictated by the representation theory of the cover.
When the covering permutations are constrained to a group $\Gamma$,
the new spectrum decomposes into isotypic blocks indexed by the non-trivial
irreducible representations of $\Gamma$ (see section \ref{sec:model}),
and the Frobenius-Schur indicator of each representation sorts its
block into one of the three classical ensembles: real representations
give real symmetric blocks (GOE, $\beta=1$), complex representations
give complex Hermitian blocks (GUE, $\beta=2$), and quaternionic
representations give quaternion self-dual blocks (GSE, $\beta=4$),
the threefold way of Dyson \cite{Dyson1962}. Our main conjecture
is that the block fluctuations realize this trichotomy.
\begin{conjecture}
\label{conj:symmetry_classes}Fix $d\geq3$ and a finite group $\Gamma$,
let $\rho$ be a non-trivial irreducible representation of $\Gamma$
with Frobenius-Schur indicator $\varepsilon(\rho)\in\{1,0,-1\}$,
and let $A_{\rho}$ denote the $\rho$-isotypic block of a uniformly
random $\Gamma$-cover of a random simple $d$-regular base graph
on $n$ vertices. Then there is a constant $c_{\rho}>0$ such that
\[
\frac{\lambda_{\max}(A_{\rho})-2\sqrt{d-1}}{c_{\rho}\,n^{-2/3}}\rightarrow TW_{\beta}
\]
in distribution as $n\rightarrow\infty$, where $\beta=1,2,4$ according
as $\varepsilon(\rho)=1,0,-1$, and $TW_{\beta}$ is the Tracy-Widom
law of the corresponding ensemble in its original normalization. In
particular the centering is exactly $2\sqrt{d-1}$, with no shift.
\end{conjecture}
The largest new eigenvalue of the cover is the maximum of the block
maxima, and blocks of representations that are not complex conjugates
of one another behave independently in our data. This yields explicit
limiting Ramanujan probabilities.
\begin{conjecture}
\label{conj:ramanujan_probabilities}With the notation of Conjecture
\ref{conj:symmetry_classes}, the block maxima $\lambda_{\max}(A_{\rho})$
are asymptotically independent across non-conjugate non-trivial representations,
and the probability that a random $\Gamma$-cover is one-sided Ramanujan
satisfies
\[
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P\left(\lambda_{\max}(A_{\rho})\leq2\sqrt{d-1}\text{ for all }\rho\right)=\prod_{[\rho]\neq1}F_{\beta(\rho)}(0),
\]
where the product runs over the non-trivial irreducible representations
of $\Gamma$ up to complex conjugation, and $F_{\beta}$ is the Tracy-Widom
distribution function. Here $F_{1}(0)=0.8319081\dots$, $F_{2}(0)=0.96937\dots$,
and $F_{4}(0)=0.99857\dots$.
\end{conjecture}
When all the non-trivial blocks share one symmetry class the largest
new eigenvalue itself is Tracy-Widom, and this is what the data shows:
$\mathbb{Z}_{3}$ covers follow $TW_{2}$, quaternion covers follow
$TW_{4}$, and permutation covers, whose new spectrum is the single
block of the real standard representation of $S_{k}$, follow $TW_{1}$.
When the blocks mix classes no single Tracy-Widom law can appear,
and the data confirms this too: the largest new eigenvalue of a $\mathbb{Z}_{4}$
cover, a maximum of one GOE block and one GUE block, and of a $\mathbb{Z}_{5}$
cover, a maximum of two independent GUE blocks, each drift away from
every Tracy-Widom law, while matching the predicted maximum law to
three decimal places (see Table \ref{tab:predicted_observed} in section
\ref{sec:covers}). Our final conjecture extends the picture to covers
of a fixed irregular base graph, where no theorem and, to our knowledge,
no prior experiment exists.
\begin{conjecture}
\label{conj:irregular_base}Let $B$ be a fixed connected base graph
of minimum degree at least $3$, let $\rho(B)$ denote the spectral radius of
its universal cover, and let $H_{k}$ be a uniformly random degree
$k$ cover of $B$. Then there is a constant $c_{B}>0$ such that
\[
\frac{\lambda_{new}(H_{k})-\rho(B)}{c_{B}\,k^{-2/3}}\rightarrow TW_{1}
\]
in distribution as $k\rightarrow\infty$, where $\lambda_{new}$ is
the largest new eigenvalue. In particular
\[
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}P\left(H_{k}\text{ is one-sided Ramanujan}\right)=F_{1}(0)=0.8319081\dots
\]
for every such base graph $B$.
\end{conjecture}
The $\beta=1$ law in Conjecture \ref{conj:irregular_base} is forced
by the picture above, since the standard representation of $S_{k}$
is real for every $k$. Taking $B$ to be the bouquet of two loops
recovers the $d=4$ case of Theorem \ref{thm:hmy} (in the permutation
model), and so Conjecture \ref{conj:irregular_base} is the common
generalization of the proven regular case to arbitrary base graphs.
Both cases are tested in section \ref{sec:irregular} at five million
covers per size of the fixed bases $K_{5}$ and $K_{5}-e$: each family
selects the $TW_{1}$ law, and the empirical Ramanujan probabilities
at $V=10{,}000$ are $0.83281$ and $0.82916$ against the conjectured
$F_{1}(0)=0.8319081\dots$.

The conjectures above date to 2018 and were formulated jointly with
Lior Silberman, following a suggestion of Peter Sarnak. All of the
data reported here was generated by 2023, before the appearance of
\cite{HuangMcKenzieYau2024}, with every family but the two fixed base
ones predating \cite{HuangYau2023} as well, and so the pipeline was
in no way tuned to the theorems it now confirms. This timing shapes how the
paper should be read: the regular graph families of section \ref{sec:regular}
are no longer evidence for a conjecture but a control experiment, and the value
of that section lies in what the theorem does not address, namely
the finite size behavior, the diagnosis of MNS's $52\%$, and the
failure of the multigraph model. The cover families of sections \ref{sec:covers}
and \ref{sec:irregular} are the substance of the paper.

We stress three differences between our experiment and that of MNS.
First, scale: we use $5\times10^{6}$ samples per size against their
$1000$, sizes up to $V=500{,}000$ against their $20{,}000$, and
$1.3\times10^{8}$ samples in total, so that the four candidate laws,
whose distribution functions differ only in the third decimal, are
separated by dozens of binomial standard errors rather than fractions
of one. Second, methodology: instead of fitting a single power law
across all sizes we track the local, consecutive size exponents of
the mean gap and standard deviation, which reveals that a global fit
over $V\leq20{,}000$ systematically overestimates the mean exponent,
the precise finite size artifact behind (\ref{eq:mns_52_percent}).
Third, and principally, scope: the covers of sections \ref{sec:covers}
and \ref{sec:irregular}, with their representation theoretic selection
of the symmetry class, have no analog in MNS or, to our knowledge,
in any published experiment. Throughout, we study the one-sided question
only, and make no claims about the joint behavior of the smallest
new eigenvalue.

\section{\label{sec:model}The Numerical Model and the Dataset}

\subsection{Random Covers and the Isotypic Decomposition}

Let $B$ be a connected base graph on $n$ vertices, possibly with
loops and multiple edges. A degree $k$ cover of $B$ is determined
by a voltage assignment $\sigma$ giving each oriented edge $e=(u,v)$
of $B$ a permutation $\sigma_{e}\in S_{k}$, with $\sigma_{\bar{e}}=\sigma_{e}^{-1}$
on the reversed edge: the cover $H$ has vertex set $V(B)\times\{1,\dots,k\}$,
with $(u,i)$ adjacent to $(v,\sigma_{e}(i))$ for each edge $e=(u,v)$.
A \emph{random cover} of degree $k$ assigns independent uniform permutations
to the edges of $B$. More generally, given a finite group $\Gamma$
of order $k$, a random $\Gamma$-cover assigns independent uniform
elements of $\Gamma$ to the edges, acting on the fiber $\Gamma$
by right multiplication. The eigenvalues of $B$ lift to $H$, and
are called the old eigenvalues, and the remaining $n(k-1)$ eigenvalues
are called \emph{new}, and we write $\lambda_{new}(H)$ for the largest
new eigenvalue.

The spectrum of a $\Gamma$-cover organizes along the irreducible
representations of $\Gamma$. Decomposing the permutation action
of $\Gamma$ on its group algebra into irreducibles, the adjacency
matrix of $H$ is conjugate to a direct sum
\begin{equation}
A_{H}\cong\bigoplus_{\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(\Gamma)}A_{\rho}^{\oplus d_{\rho}},\ \ \ A_{\rho}=\sum_{e=(u,v)}E_{uv}\otimes\rho(\sigma_{e}),\label{eq:isotypic_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where the sum runs over the oriented edges of $B$, $E_{uv}$ is the
matrix unit, and $d_{\rho}$ is the dimension of $\rho$, so that
$A_{\rho}$ is an $nd_{\rho}$ dimensional Hermitian matrix (see \cite[Section 2]{HallPuderSawin2018}
for this decomposition). The trivial representation recovers the base,
$A_{1}=A_{B}$, and so the new spectrum of $H$ is the union of the
spectra of the non-trivial blocks $A_{\rho}$, each with multiplicity
$d_{\rho}$. Note that the blocks of complex conjugate representations
satisfy $A_{\bar{\rho}}=\overline{A_{\rho}}$ and have identical spectra,
and so the distinct sources of randomness are indexed by the non-trivial
representations up to conjugation. For a full symmetric cover the
same decomposition applies with $\Gamma=S_{k}$ acting on $\{1,\dots,k\}$:
the permutation representation splits as the trivial plus the standard
$(k-1)$ dimensional representation, and the new spectrum is the single
block of the standard representation.

The Frobenius-Schur indicator
\[
\varepsilon(\rho)=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\chi_{\rho}(g^{2})\in\{1,0,-1\}
\]
sorts the irreducible representations into real ($\varepsilon=1$),
complex ($\varepsilon=0$), and quaternionic ($\varepsilon=-1$) types,
and correspondingly sorts the blocks $A_{\rho}$ into the three classical
matrix symmetry classes: a real representation may be realized over
$\mathbb{R}$, making $A_{\rho}$ conjugate to a real symmetric matrix,
a complex representation gives a complex Hermitian block with no further
symmetry, and a quaternionic representation gives a self-dual block
whose eigenvalues carry an even multiplicity. In random matrix theory
these are the symmetry classes of the GOE, GUE, and GSE, whose largest
eigenvalues fluctuate according to the Tracy-Widom laws $TW_{1}$,
$TW_{2}$, and $TW_{4}$ \cite{TracyWidom1994,TracyWidom1996}. This
threefold way \cite{Dyson1962} is the mechanism behind Conjecture
\ref{conj:symmetry_classes}. Our families realize all three types:
for $\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ the non-trivial characters $\chi_{j}(x)=e^{2\pi ijx/k}$
give $n$ dimensional blocks with entries $\chi_{j}(\sigma_{e})$
on the edges of the base, complex Hermitian in general and real symmetric
exactly when $\chi_{j}=\pm1$ is real valued, while the quaternion
group $Q_{8}$ has a unique non-trivial higher dimensional representation,
the two dimensional representation of quaternionic type, whose real
form is four dimensional.

Concretely, $\mathbb{Z}_{3}$ has one conjugate pair of complex characters,
and so the largest new eigenvalue of a random $\mathbb{Z}_{3}$ cover
is the largest eigenvalue of a single GUE class block. $\mathbb{Z}_{4}$
has the real character $\chi_{2}=(-1)^{x}$ together with one conjugate
pair, and so its largest new eigenvalue is the maximum of a GOE class
and a GUE class maximum. $\mathbb{Z}_{5}$ has two conjugate pairs,
giving the maximum of two GUE class maxima with identical marginal
distributions. The quaternion family samples the four dimensional
real form of the quaternionic block of a random $Q_{8}$-cover directly,
a $4n$ dimensional matrix all of whose eigenvalues are new, with
each eigenvalue doubled by the quaternionic structure. These five
families, together with the permutation covers of the bouquet (random
$4$-regular graphs), of $K_{5}$, and of $K_{5}$ minus an edge, are the subject
of sections \ref{sec:regular} through \ref{sec:irregular}.

\subsection{\label{subsec:statistics}The Statistics and the Tracy-Widom Constants}

By $TW_{\beta}$ we mean the Tracy-Widom law in the original normalization
of Tracy and Widom \cite{TracyWidom1994,TracyWidom1996}, with distribution
function $F_{\beta}$, mean $\mu_{\beta}$, and standard deviation
$\sigma_{\beta}$, and by $\widetilde{TW}_{\beta}$ the standardization
to mean $0$ and variance $1$. The constants we use are
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{ccccc}
\beta & \mu_{\beta} & \sigma_{\beta} & \left|\mu_{\beta}\right|/\sigma_{\beta} & F_{\beta}(\mu_{\beta})\\
1 & -1.2065335746 & 1.2679830577 & 0.9515376 & 0.519652\\
2 & -1.7710868074 & 0.9017731382 & 1.9640048 & 0.515016\\
4 & -2.3068848932 & 0.7195302084 & 3.2060987 & 0.511069
\end{array}\label{eq:tw_constants}
\end{equation}
following \cite{Bornemann2010Fredholm,Bornemann2010Review}, and the
values $F_{1}(0)=0.8319081\dots$, $F_{2}(0)=0.96937\dots$, $F_{4}(0)=0.99857\dots$
of the distribution functions at the origin.

For each family and size we record the sample mean $\mu_{V}$, standard
deviation $\sigma_{V}$, skewness, and excess kurtosis of the largest
new eigenvalue $\lambda$, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between
the standardized sample and each of $\widetilde{TW}_{1},\widetilde{TW}_{2},\widetilde{TW}_{4}$
and the standard normal, the fraction of samples below the Ramanujan
threshold, and the fraction below the sample mean. The last statistic
is MNS's discriminator: the standardized Tracy-Widom laws are nearly
indistinguishable by their overall shape, however their masses to
the left of the mean, listed in (\ref{eq:tw_constants}), differ in
the third decimal, and with $10^{6}$ to $5\times10^{6}$ samples
the binomial standard error is between $2\times10^{-4}$ and $5\times10^{-4}$, separating
the four candidates by tens of standard errors.

The relevance of the ratio $\left(2\sqrt{d-1}-\mu_{V}\right)/\sigma_{V}$
is the following elementary observation of MNS. Writing $g_{V}$ for
this ratio, the probability of being one-sided Ramanujan is
\[
P\left(\lambda\leq2\sqrt{d-1}\right)=P\left(\frac{\lambda-\mu_{V}}{\sigma_{V}}\leq g_{V}\right),
\]
and so if the standardized law converges to $\widetilde{TW}_{1}$,
then
\begin{equation}
\lim_{V\rightarrow\infty}P\left(\lambda\leq2\sqrt{d-1}\right)=\begin{cases}
F_{1}(\mu_{TW_{1}})=0.519652\dots & \text{if }g_{V}\rightarrow0\\
\widetilde{F}_{1}(r) & \text{if }g_{V}\rightarrow r\in(0,\infty)\\
1 & \text{if }g_{V}\rightarrow\infty
\end{cases}.\label{eq:trichotomy}
\end{equation}
MNS's power law fits pointed to the first case. The Tracy-Widom scaling
(\ref{eq:hmy_constants}), with no shift in the centering, forces
the middle case with $r=\left|\mu_{TW_{1}}\right|/\sigma_{TW_{1}}$,
where $\widetilde{F}_{1}(r)=F_{1}(0)$, and this is what our data
shows and what Theorem \ref{thm:hmy} now proves.

\subsection{\label{subsec:dataset}The Dataset and the Pipeline}

The dataset consists of $1.3\times10^{8}$ samples across nine series,
each sample the largest new eigenvalue of one independently generated
graph or cover, all with base degree $d=4$.

\hline
family & sizes & samples & model\tabularnewline
\hline

The simple $4$-regular model draws two independent uniform permutations
$\pi_{1},\pi_{2}$ of $\{1,\dots,V\}$ and forms the graph with adjacency
matrix $P_{1}+P_{1}^{T}+P_{2}+P_{2}^{T}$, resampling locally until
the result has no loops or multiple edges, and discarding the rare
disconnected samples. This is the permutation model of MNS's family
$G_{V,4}$ conditioned on simplicity, equivalently a random degree
$V$ cover of the bouquet of two loops. Conditioned on simplicity
it is contiguous with the uniform simple $4$-regular model, see \cite{GreenhillJansonKimWormald2002}
and the survey \cite{Wormald1999}, and so Theorem \ref{thm:hmy}
applies. The permutation multigraph series keeps the loops and multiple
edges, with a loop contributing $2$ to the diagonal. The abelian
series draw a fresh random simple $4$-regular base graph on $n$
vertices for each batch, assign each base edge an independent uniform
voltage in $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$, and record the largest new eigenvalue
of the explicit $kn$-vertex cover, ten covers per base graph. A one-way
analysis of variance across the ten covers sharing a base gives intraclass
correlations below $3\times10^{-4}$ in absolute value at the largest
sizes, and below $2.4\times10^{-3}$ at the smallest, and so the residual randomness of the
base is invisible at the fluctuation scale and the samples may be
treated as independent. The quaternion series assigns each edge of
a random simple $4$-regular base an independent uniform element of
$Q_{8}$ and records the top eigenvalue of the resulting $4n$ dimensional
real block. The two fixed base series assign each edge of $K_{5}$,
respectively of $K_{5}$ minus an edge, an independent uniform permutation
in $S_{k}$, and record the largest new eigenvalue of the cover on
$5k$ vertices, with the five lifted base eigenvalues removed exactly.
Both families are analyzed in section \ref{sec:irregular}.

Eigenvalues are computed with the implicitly restarted Lanczos method
(ARPACK), applied to the adjacency matrix shifted by a small multiple
of the identity so that the algebraically largest eigenvalues dominate
in magnitude. Old eigenvalues are removed by comparing against the
base spectrum, computed exactly, with a tolerance of $10^{-10}$.
For the regular families this removes the trivial eigenvalue $d$
and yields $\lambda_{2}$. The number of computed eigenvalues per
graph starts at $2$ and grows automatically whenever every computed
eigenvalue is old. Two precision caveats apply. The legacy series
were generated years earlier in MATLAB and are recorded on a $10^{-12}$
grid, coarser than machine precision. A per process seeding scheme
caused a small number of duplicated batches within the abelian series.
Comparing duplicated batches shows agreement to roughly $10^{-8}$,
which we take as the effective reproducibility of the iterative eigensolver,
and none of the statistics reported here move at the quoted precision
when duplicates are dropped. All summary statistics were verified
by independent recomputation from the raw samples.

\section{\label{sec:regular}Random $4$-Regular Graphs}

In this section we test the pipeline against Theorem \ref{thm:hmy}
along the two simple $4$-regular series, diagnose the finite size
artifact behind MNS's $52\%$, and isolate a model, the permutation
multigraph, which falls outside the universality class. Since the
data predates \cite{HuangYau2023,HuangMcKenzieYau2024}, none of what
follows was tuned to the answers.

\subsection{Convergence to the Proven Law}

Figure \ref{fig:pdf_simple} shows the standardized empirical density

candidate laws, and Figure \ref{fig:qq_matlab} the quantile-quantile

\subsection{\label{subsec:mns_diagnosis}The Origin of the $52\%$ Conjecture}

MNS reached the first case of (\ref{eq:trichotomy}) by fitting single
power laws to the mean gap and standard deviation over their full
range of sizes. Our data reproduces their fit and shows exactly how
it misleads. Fitting one line to $\log\left(2\sqrt{3}-\mu_{V}\right)$
against $\log V$ over our eight sizes $V\leq20{,}000$, the MNS range,
gives a slope of $-0.787$, while the same fit to $\log\sigma_{V}$
gives $-0.684$: a mean exponent apparently steeper than the standard
deviation exponent, exactly the pattern from which MNS concluded $g_{V}\rightarrow0$
and hence $52\%$, with numbers closely matching their $d=3$ values
$m\approx-0.79$ and $s\approx-0.72$. The local exponents tell a
different story. Figure \ref{fig:local_exponent} plots the slope
between consecutive sizes: the standard deviation exponent sits within
$0.01$ of $-2/3$ from $V\approx2000$ onward, while the mean gap
exponent drifts monotonically from $-0.90$ at $V\approx100$ through
$-0.72$ at $V=20{,}000$ to $-0.70$ over the last legacy pair at
$V=500{,}000$, still descending toward $-2/3$. A single fitted line
over small sizes averages this drift and reports a transient. MNS
saw the warning sign, observing that their best fit exponents increased
monotonically as sizes were added, and were appropriately cautious. At
$V\leq20{,}000$ the data could do no better. The ratio $g_{V}$
resolves the question directly: as Figure \ref{fig:ratio} shows,
$g_{V}$ does not tend to $0$ for any family, and for the simple
$4$-regular series it descends to $0.9993$ at $V=500{,}000$, approaching
the Tracy-Widom ratio $\left|\mu_{TW_{1}}\right|/\sigma_{TW_{1}}=0.951537\dots$
from above, in agreement with the middle case of (\ref{eq:trichotomy})
and with $F_{1}(0)=0.8319081\dots$ as the limiting Ramanujan probability.

\subsection{\label{subsec:multigraph}Failure of the Multigraph Model}

MNS pooled models with and without loops and multiple edges, observing
similar behavior at their sample sizes. At ours, the difference is
qualitative. The permutation multigraph series, identical to the legacy
simple series except that loops and multiple edges are kept, has Kolmogorov-Smirnov
distance to every candidate law growing with $V$, reaching $0.085$
at $V=500{,}000$, sample skewness $74.5$, and excess kurtosis $1.15\times10^{4}$,
against the $TW_{1}$ values $0.293$ and $0.165$. Figure \ref{fig:tail}
shows the mechanism: the right tail of the simple model dies within
$10^{-4}$ of the threshold $2\sqrt{3}$, while the multigraph model
has a heavy tail of far outliers, reaching $\lambda=3.538$ at $V=50{,}000$
and containing roughly $17\%$ of its mass above $2\sqrt{3}$ at $V=500{,}000$.
These outliers are localized eigenvalues seeded by loops and multiple edges,
whose density vanishes in the limit but whose spectral effect does
not vanish at the $V^{-2/3}$ fluctuation scale. The lesson for experiments
in this area is that the universality class is a property of the conditioned
model: the simple and multigraph models share the same limiting spectral
distribution and the same almost sure convergence of $\lambda_{2}$
to $2\sqrt{3}$, and are separated only at the fluctuation scale,
where one is Tracy-Widom and the other is not even tight around the
threshold in units of its own standard deviation, its ratio $g_{V}$
falling through $0.67$ with no sign of stabilizing.

\section{\label{sec:covers}Covers and the Threefold Way}

We now turn to the main experiment. Each abelian and quaternion family
isolates a definite set of isotypic blocks, and Conjecture \ref{conj:symmetry_classes}
predicts the symmetry class of each: $\mathbb{Z}_{3}$ covers should
follow $\widetilde{TW}_{2}$, quaternion covers $\widetilde{TW}_{4}$,
the regular graph families of section \ref{sec:regular} $\widetilde{TW}_{1}$,
and the mixed families $\mathbb{Z}_{4}$ and $\mathbb{Z}_{5}$ no
single Tracy-Widom law at all.

\subsection{Ensemble Selection in the Pure Families}

Figure \ref{fig:ks_classes} is the structural result of this paper:
for each pure family, the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance to the law of
its own symmetry class falls to the sampling floor while the distance
to $\widetilde{TW}_{1}$ (dotted) stalls. At the largest sizes, the
$\mathbb{Z}_{3}$ family reaches distance $1.0\times10^{-3}$ from
$\widetilde{TW}_{2}$ (floor at $10^{6}$ samples: roughly $9\times10^{-4}$)
while its distance to $\widetilde{TW}_{1}$ is stuck at $5.4\times10^{-3}$,
and the quaternion family reaches $7.5\times10^{-4}$ from $\widetilde{TW}_{4}$
with distance $8.8\times10^{-3}$ to $\widetilde{TW}_{1}$. The mass
to the left of the mean (Figure \ref{fig:mass_left}) separates the
classes at higher resolution: the $\mathbb{Z}_{3}$ series converges
to $0.5145$ against the $TW_{2}$ value $0.515016$, the quaternion
series to $0.5109$ against the $TW_{4}$ value $0.511069$, and the
regular series to $0.5201$ against the $TW_{1}$ value $0.519652$,
each matching its own class, excluding every competing class by six
or more binomial standard errors. The affine invariant moments agree: Figure
\ref{fig:moments} shows the sample skewness and excess kurtosis of
each family converging to the values of its own class, $\left(0.213,0.093\right)$
at the largest $\mathbb{Z}_{3}$ size against the $TW_{2}$ values
$\left(0.224,0.093\right)$, and $\left(0.156,0.042\right)$ at the
largest quaternion size against the $TW_{4}$ values $\left(0.166,0.049\right)$.
Figure \ref{fig:universality} overlays the standardized densities
of the largest datasets from four families on the $\widetilde{TW}_{1}$
density: at the resolution of the eye all collapse to the Tracy-Widom
shape, and the discrimination between the $\beta$ classes lives in
the statistics above.

\subsection{\label{subsec:mixed}The Mixed Families and the Maximum Law}

The largest new eigenvalue of a $\mathbb{Z}_{4}$ cover is the maximum
of a GOE class maximum and a GUE class maximum, and that of a $\mathbb{Z}_{5}$
cover is the maximum of two GUE class maxima, and so under Conjectures
\ref{conj:symmetry_classes} and \ref{conj:ramanujan_probabilities}
neither should converge to any single Tracy-Widom law. This is visible
in the raw statistics: the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance of the $\mathbb{Z}_{4}$
family to every candidate law grows with $V$, reaching $1.9\times10^{-2}$
against $\widetilde{TW}_{1}$ at the largest size, and the $\mathbb{Z}_{5}$
family, nearest $\widetilde{TW}_{1}$ at moderate sizes, drifts away
from it as $V$ grows.

The maximum structure can be tested quantitatively. If the two blocks
of a family fluctuate independently with the same centering $2\sqrt{d-1}$
and the same scale constant, then the standardized largest new eigenvalue
converges to the standardized law of
\[
M=\max\left(X_{1},X_{2}\right),
\]
where $X_{1},X_{2}$ are independent Tracy-Widom variables of the
appropriate classes on a common scale. Computing the distribution
function $F_{1}F_{2}$ (for $\mathbb{Z}_{4}$) and $F_{2}^{2}$ (for
$\mathbb{Z}_{5}$) numerically gives the predicted constants in Table
\ref{tab:predicted_observed}: the ratio $\left|\mu_{M}\right|/\sigma_{M}$,
the mass of $M$ left of its mean, and the Ramanujan functional $F_{\beta_{1}}(0)F_{\beta_{2}}(0)$.
The $\mathbb{Z}_{5}$ family, whose two blocks have identical marginal
distributions and for which the equal scale hypothesis is automatic,
matches all three predictions to three decimal places at $n=5000$.
The $\mathbb{Z}_{4}$ family, whose blocks belong to different classes
and converge at visibly different rates, is still drifting at $n=5000$,
in each statistic monotonically toward the predicted value.

\subsection{\label{subsec:amplitudes}Scaling Amplitudes}

All nine series share the $V^{-2/3}$ rate, with family dependent
amplitudes (Figures \ref{fig:compensated} and \ref{fig:scaling_abelian}).
The fixed base families are treated in section \ref{sec:irregular}.
For the abelian families the amplitudes themselves appear to carry
no new constant: each character block $A_{\chi}$ is the adjacency
matrix of the random base graph with its entries rotated by the unimodular
phases $\chi(\sigma_{e})$, a perturbation which does not change the
tree-like local structure, and so we conjecture that every non-trivial
character block obeys Conjecture \ref{conj:symmetry_classes} with
the same constant $c_{\chi}=c(d)$ as the unphased regular graph,
where $c(d)$ is defined in (\ref{eq:hmy_constants}) and $n$ is
the base size. The data supports this to about one percent at $n=5000$.
For $\mathbb{Z}_{3}$ the prediction $\sigma\approx\sigma_{TW_{2}}c(4)n^{-2/3}=2.568\times10^{-3}$
compares to the measured $2.590\times10^{-3}$, and the predicted
mean gap $\left|\mu_{TW_{2}}\right|c(4)n^{-2/3}=5.044\times10^{-3}$
to the measured $5.017\times10^{-3}$. For $\mathbb{Z}_{5}$, using
the maximum law of Table \ref{tab:predicted_observed}, the predicted
standard deviation and gap are $2.218\times10^{-3}$ and $3.598\times10^{-3}$
against the measured $2.230\times10^{-3}$ and $3.623\times10^{-3}$.
The quaternion family plateaus at an amplitude a clean factor below
the analogous prediction: the measured compensated standard deviation
$\sigma_{V}\,n^{2/3}$ drifts toward $0.534\approx\sigma_{TW_{4}}c(4)2^{-1/6}$
rather than $\sigma_{TW_{4}}c(4)=0.599$, suggesting that the correct
effective size for the quaternionic block differs from $n$ by a fixed
power of $2$, a bookkeeping familiar from the conventions of the
GSE. We do not regard the exponent $-1/6$ as established.

\section{\label{sec:irregular}Covers of an Irregular Base Graph}

Nothing in Conjecture \ref{conj:irregular_base} requires the base
graph to be regular, and in the irregular case nothing
is proven: the largest new eigenvalue should concentrate
at the spectral radius $\rho(B)$ of the universal cover, an algebraic
number lying between $2\sqrt{\delta-1}$ and $2\sqrt{\Delta-1}$, where
$\delta$ and $\Delta$ are the minimum and maximum degrees of $B$, with $TW_{1}$
fluctuations at scale $k^{-2/3}$ and limiting Ramanujan probability
$F_{1}(0)$, universally in the base graph.

Our base is $K_{5}-e$, the complete graph on five vertices minus
an edge, one of the smallest irregular base graphs of minimum degree three. Its spectrum is $\left\{ 1\pm\sqrt{7},-1,-1,0\right\} $,
with Perron eigenvalue $1+\sqrt{7}=3.6457\dots$, and since the largest
old eigenvalue exceeds $\rho(K_{5}-e)$, the largest new eigenvalue
of a cover is not even its second largest eigenvalue, so that the
exact removal of the lifted base spectrum matters. By a result of
McKay \cite{McKay2023}, $\rho(K_{5}-e)$ is the largest zero of
\[
x^{14}-20x^{12}+122x^{10}-152x^{8}-1295x^{6}+4540x^{4}-5948x^{2}-2000,
\]
an irreducible polynomial with no expression in radicals, giving
\[
\rho(K_{5}-e)=3.26287646593635862827\dots,
\]
which we re-derived and verified independently. Note the contrast
with the regular case, where $\rho=2\sqrt{d-1}$: for a general base
even the centering of the conjectured law is a non-trivial algebraic
quantity (see \cite{AngelFriedmanHoory2015,McKay2023} on computing
$\rho$, and \cite{CrawfordMarchetteMaxwellMendelson2023} for related
spectral experiments on structured random graphs).

Both families were sampled at the scale of the strongest regular
graph data: $5\times10^{6}$ independent uniform covers per size, at
cover degrees $20\leq k\leq2000$, giving graphs on $V=5k$ between
$100$ and $10{,}000$ vertices. The covers of $K_{5}$ are $4$-regular
graphs with Ramanujan threshold $2\sqrt{3}$, of a different fine
structure than the permutation model, since the vertex set carries
a fixed partition into five independent fibers with edges only between
fibers adjacent in $K_{5}$, and so they test whether the limit law
and its constants depend on anything beyond the degree. The covers
of $K_{5}-e$ test Conjecture \ref{conj:irregular_base} where no theorem
reaches, around the algebraic centering $\rho(K_{5}-e)$.

\par\end{centering}
\caption{\label{fig:ks_k5}Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance of the standardized
largest new eigenvalue to the four candidate laws for covers of $K_{5}-e$
(filled markers, standardized around $\rho(K_{5}-e)$) and of $K_{5}$

Figure \ref{fig:ks_k5} shows the outcome of the distributional test.
For both families the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance to $\widetilde{TW}_{1}$
falls monotonically with $V$, reaching $8.9\times10^{-4}$ for $K_{5}$
and $9.4\times10^{-4}$ for $K_{5}-e$ at $V=10{,}000$, at the sampling
floor and equal to the simple $4$-regular family's distance at the
same size, while the distances to the other laws stall or grow, ending
at $4.0\times10^{-3}$ ($\widetilde{TW}_{2}$), $7.9\times10^{-3}$
($\widetilde{TW}_{4}$), and $1.9\times10^{-2}$ (normal) for $K_{5}-e$.
Figure \ref{fig:pdf_k5e} shows the standardized density of the largest
$K_{5}-e$ dataset. The mass left of the mean reaches $0.51891$ ($K_{5}$)
and $0.51890$ ($K_{5}-e$) against the $TW_{1}$ value $0.519652$,
on the same convergence path as the simple family at equal sizes,
and the ratio $g_{V}$ reaches $0.9551$ and $0.9387$ against the
$TW_{1}$ ratio $0.951537\dots$ (Figure \ref{fig:ratio}). The local
exponents of the mean gap and standard deviation descend to $-0.673$
and $-0.676$ for $K_{5}$ and $-0.670$ and $-0.678$ for $K_{5}-e$
over the last pair of sizes, approaching $-2/3$ exactly as for the
regular families (Figures \ref{fig:local_exponent} and \ref{fig:scaling_k5}).
The empirical Ramanujan probabilities at $V=10{,}000$ are
\[
P\left(\lambda_{new}\leq2\sqrt{3}\right)=0.83281,\ \ \ P\left(\lambda_{new}\leq\rho(K_{5}-e)\right)=0.82916,
\]
against the conjectured $F_{1}(0)=0.8319081\dots$ (Table \ref{tab:predicted_observed}).
Every statistic selects the $\beta=1$ class, as the reality of the
standard representation predicts, and for $K_{5}-e$ it does so around
a centering which is not $2\sqrt{d-1}$ for any $d$.

Two finer observations. First, the speed of convergence depends strongly
on the base graph: the $K_{5}$ family sits within $10^{-3}$ of the
limiting Ramanujan probability from $V=1000$ onward ($0.83258$ at
$V=1000$), while the permutation model is still at $0.87282$ at
$V=10{,}000$, and the $K_{5}$ ratio $g_{V}$ is already $0.9522$
at $V=1000$ against the permutation model's $1.148$ at ten times
that size. The limiting constants are universal in the base graph,
however the finite size corrections are not, and the fixed base appears
to suppress them. In the same direction, the compensated amplitudes
of the $K_{5}$ family descend to $1.018$ and $1.066$ at $V=10{,}000$,
approaching the regular graph constants $1.0047$ and $1.0558$ of
(\ref{eq:d4_amplitudes}), and so the amplitude constant of Conjecture
\ref{conj:irregular_base} for $K_{5}$ appears to be $c(4)$ itself,
in the convention of (\ref{eq:hmy_constants}) with $V$ the cover
size. The $K_{5}-e$ amplitudes plateau lower, with $\sigma_{V}V^{2/3}$
descending through $0.893$ and the compensated gap through $0.839$
at $V=10{,}000$, and so the $K_{5}-e$ analog of $c(d)$ is a new
constant, approximately $0.70$ against $c(4)=0.8327$, the first
measurement of an edge scaling constant for an irregular base graph.

Second, the third and fourth moments tell a different story, and the
accounting is instructive. The excess kurtosis of the $K_{5}-e$ family
grows with $V$, from $0.26$ at $V=100$ to $2.52$ at $V=10{,}000$
against the $TW_{1}$ value $0.165$, and its skewness drifts upward
through $0.361$ against $0.293$, while the same statistics for $K_{5}$
are $0.28$ and $0.292$. Figure \ref{fig:tail_k5} locates the cause
in a thin tail of far outliers. At $V=10{,}000$ a fraction $9\times10^{-6}$
of the $K_{5}-e$ samples lie above $\rho+10\sigma_{V}$, and the
extreme sample sits at $\lambda=3.3424$, roughly $42$ standard deviations
above the mean, while the extreme $K_{5}$ sample sits $21$ standard
deviations out and the extreme sample of the simple $4$-regular family
only $9$. In units of $\sigma_{V}$ the $K_{5}-e$ tail lengthens
with $V$, from $21$ standard deviations at $V=1000$ to $42$ at
$V=10{,}000$, the signature of events whose probability decays polynomially
rather than exponentially: the relative Alon theorem \cite{Bordenave2020}
is an asymptotically almost sure statement, and rare local structures
in a random cover can push a new eigenvalue beyond $\rho+\epsilon$
at a small polynomial frequency (compare the role of tangles in \cite{Friedman2008}).
These outliers are the multigraph phenomenon of section \ref{subsec:multigraph}
in miniature. They dominate the third and fourth moments, which is
why the fixed base families are omitted from Figure \ref{fig:moments},
however at frequency $10^{-5}$ they are invisible to every quantile
level statistic, moving the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance by at most
their total mass and the Ramanujan probability by even less. The bulk
law is $TW_{1}$, and the tail rides on top of it.

\section{\label{sec:open_problems}Open Problems}

\subsection*{The Block Laws}

Conjecture \ref{conj:symmetry_classes} is open in every case, including
$\beta=1$: for covers of a $d$-regular base of size $n\geq2$ no
analog of Theorem \ref{thm:hmy} is known, and for irregular bases
even the correct formulation of edge rigidity around $\rho(B)$ is
open. The abelian case seems the natural first target, since the block
$A_{\chi}$ is an explicit random Hermitian matrix built from a random
regular graph and iid phases.
\begin{problem}
\label{prob:abelian_block}Prove that the largest eigenvalue of the
$\mathbb{Z}_{3}$-character block $A_{\chi}$ of a random $\mathbb{Z}_{3}$-cover
of a random simple $d$-regular graph has $TW_{2}$ fluctuations,
in the sense of Conjecture \ref{conj:symmetry_classes}.
\end{problem}

\subsection*{The Maximum Laws and the Amplitudes}

The mixed families raise a cleaner question than convergence alone:
the maximum law depends on the relative scale of the blocks, which
we conjectured equal for character blocks in section \ref{subsec:amplitudes}.
\begin{problem}
\label{prob:amplitudes}Determine the scale constants $c_{\rho}$
of Conjecture \ref{conj:symmetry_classes} and $c_{B}$ of Conjecture
\ref{conj:irregular_base}. In particular, prove that $c_{\chi}=c(d)$
for every non-trivial character $\chi$ of an abelian cover, determine
the quaternionic constant, for which the data suggests $c_{\rho}=c(d)2^{-1/6}$,
and determine $c_{K_{5}-e}$, for which the data gives approximately
$0.70$.
\end{problem}
I believe that the equal amplitude prediction $c_{\chi}=c(d)$ is
exactly right, as the one percent discrepancies at $n=5000$ shrink
monotonically with $n$ in our data.

\subsection*{Far Outliers}

The permutation multigraph model of section \ref{subsec:multigraph}
converges to the same threshold with the same $V^{-2/3}$ mean gap,
however its fluctuations are dominated by localized outliers, and
the fixed base families of section \ref{sec:irregular} carry a much
thinner tail of the same character.
\begin{problem}
\label{prob:multigraph}Determine the limiting distribution of the
largest non-trivial eigenvalue of the permutation multigraph model.
Is it a Tracy-Widom law conditioned on the absence of localized eigenvalues,
plus an independent point process of outliers?
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}
\label{prob:outlier_rate}For a fixed base graph $B$ and $\epsilon>0$,
determine the decay rate of
\[
P\left(\lambda_{new}(H_{k})>\rho(B)+\epsilon\right)
\]
for a uniformly random degree $k$ cover $H_{k}$ of $B$. The $K_{5}-e$
data is consistent with a polynomially small rate: the extreme sample
moves from $21$ to $42$ standard deviations above the mean as $V$
grows from $1000$ to $10{,}000$, at frequencies near $10^{-5}$.
\end{problem}

\subsection*{Growing Covers}

Huang and Yau \cite{HuangYau2023} proved Tracy-Widom fluctuations
for regular graphs of growing degree. The analogous regime for covers,
base size fixed and cover group growing, is precisely the setting
of Conjecture \ref{conj:irregular_base}, however one may also grow
the base: for $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ covers with $k$ growing with $n$,
the number of GUE blocks grows, and the largest new eigenvalue becomes
the maximum of a growing number of Tracy-Widom variables, suggesting
a transition from Tracy-Widom to Gumbel fluctuations.
\begin{problem}
\label{prob:gumbel}For random $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ covers of a random
$d$-regular graph on $n$ vertices, determine the joint regimes of
$(k,n)$ in which the largest new eigenvalue has Tracy-Widom, and
Gumbel, fluctuations.
\end{problem}
